# Design decisions (product owner, 2026-10-11)

The landing page must look like the prototype screenshots (`design/prototype/Main.dc.html`,
`Tracking.dc.html`, "MoversHub Platform.pdf"). Where the prototype conflicts with `COMPLIANCE.md`,
these decisions apply:

| # | Topic | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Vehicle classes | **5 classes** as in COMPLIANCE.md §1.1 (`SMALL`, `VAN`, `LARGE_VAN`, `BOX`, `TRUCK`) — not the prototype's 4. |
| 2 | Tile price | Big price like the design (**€97**) with a small **"from"** label above it (lowest mover offer in that class). |
| 3 | Banned wording (§12) | Compliant wording, same look: crew **"Mover only"** (not "Driver"), **"Payout €X"** (not "You earn"), **company name** on the live map (not "Arben K."), booking fee **shown separately** (not "Price includes MoversHub's fee"). |
| 4 | Prices before the backend exists | Show **sample prices, clearly labelled** ("Sample rates for the demo — each mover sets their own price…"), computed on the server; replaced by real server quotes later. |
| 5 | Addresses & map (2026-10-11) | Pickup / drop-off are **address search** (not city lists); the hero shows a **real map** with the real road route. Free OpenStreetMap services for now (no Google billing yet); switch to Google later without UI changes. |
| 6 | Booking fee (2026-10-11) | Amount **not decided yet**. Built as a setting (`BOOKING_FEE_RULE` in `packages/shared`: flat per currency or % of the mover's price), always shown as its own line; while it is `null` the line reads "to be announced" and nothing is charged. |

| 8 | Three apps, mandatory (2026-10-12) — **replaced by #15** | Like Lugg, the market leader: the platform is **always three apps** — the **website** (`web/app`: booking, service pages, tracking links, admin), the **customer app** (`mobile/customer`) and a separate **mover app** (`mobile/mover`). | 13 | Prices to the cent (2026-10-12) | A "from" price must **equal** the lowest mover quote (COMPLIANCE §13), so prices and payouts are never rounded for display — on the website and in both apps ("from €149.42"; whole amounts stay "€162"). Decision #2 keeps its look. |
| 14 | Whole lek, denar and dinar (2026-10-12) | Their hundredths are no longer used (no qindarka; deni coins withdrawn 2013; para gone since 2008), so the **server** prices them in whole units: every line of a quote, the commission, the payout and the booking fee (`roundToPriceStep` in `packages/shared/src/money.ts`, used by the pricing engine). The lines still add up exactly, and the shown price is the charged price. Euro, złoty and francs keep their cents. |
| 15 | Website to inform, one app to use (2026-10-13) | Replaces #8 and #12. **The website** says what MoversHub is, lets people **create an account as a customer or a mover**, and has the **download buttons**: iPhone, Android and the computer version (the app's web build, installable from the browser). It keeps the price preview; its button says "Get the app". Booking, tracking and payment happen **in the app**. The website still hosts what must be on the web: the public tracking-link page (COMPLIANCE §9.1), legal pages and "How ranking works" (§3, §8), and the admin panel. **One app** (`mobile/app`, "MoversHub") for customers and movers — two apps were harder to explain and to get people to install. It opens on **sign in / create account**; creating an account asks "I need a mover" or "I'm a mover"; the role is saved once the code has proven the email (`choose_role`, never from sign-up data) — an account without one is asked after signing in, and an email that already had the other kind of account is told so (switching sides: #16). After sign-in the role picks the customer side (prices, booking) or the dark mover side (setup, jobs). Every email gets the same answer on the sign-in screen (no account check). Sign-in by **email + 6-digit code** (since #17: email + password, a code only to confirm the email and to reset a password): the cloud project needs a domain + custom SMTP with code templates before anyone can create an account in the app against it — its built-in email sends only a link (phone codes dropped, no Twilio for sign-in). Background location is declared in the one app but asked only from movers, when a job starts — the store listings must explain it. |
| 16 | Switching sides (2026-10-13) | One account can be a customer and a mover: the home screen has **Switch to the mover side** / **Switch to the customer side** (`choose_role`; never to or from admin). Nothing is lost by switching. The mover side starts with the setup checklist: job requests come only once the company, documents, vans, rates and payouts are filled in and verified (COMPLIANCE §5, enforced in `quotable_vehicles`) — switching alone gives no work. An email that already had the other kind of account is told so after the code and pointed to the switch; the app never switches without being asked. The side is remembered on the device, so the app opens on it at once (and offline). |
| 17 | Email and password (2026-10-13) | Owner: a code only to prove the email, then people **choose a password** and sign in with **email + password**; **Forgot password?** emails a code to set a new one. **Every sign-in by code ends with choosing a password** — after "Create account" (a new account, or an address that already has one: no endless sign-in by code) and after "Forgot password?" — and until it is chosen the app shows nothing else, also after a restart (kept on the device, not in the account's metadata, which whoever creates a login can write). On the server, a password set before the address was proven is cleared when the owner confirms it with an emailed or texted code (migrations 20261013120000 and 20261013130000), so nobody can plant a password on someone else's address; a login an admin creates already confirmed keeps the password the admin set (no code was sent). Rules: at least 8 characters with a letter a–z and a digit, at most 72 bytes — Supabase Auth checks them (`minimum_password_length` 8, `password_requirements` letters_digits: set the same in the cloud project) and the app checks them first. Every address gets the same answers: a wrong password and an unknown address say the same, and "Forgot password?" / "Create account" show the code screen also when Supabase Auth refuses a second email to an existing address within a minute (its API itself still answers differently there; only a server in between could hide that). Errors are shown only when every address would get them: no answer at all, too many attempts from one network and, on "Create account", the project's hourly email limit (a new address is emailed too) — not on "Forgot password?", where Supabase Auth answers an unknown address before it counts emails. Emails: "Your MoversHub code" (confirm, or sign in) and "Reset your MoversHub password", with the code (local templates; the cloud needs custom SMTP, step 4). The website's sign-in follows in its step 2, the same way. |

| 19 | Mover setup, step 1: "Your company" (2026-10-14) | The first step of the mover's setup checklist is a screen of its own (app route `/mover/company`, dark mover side): **business** (company or registered sole trader — preferred, COMPLIANCE §5) or **private person**; the **country of registration** first (the markets MoversHub prices: XK, AL, MK, PL, DE, AT, CH), then the legal name, numbers, the country where they pay tax (DAC7; follows the registration country unless changed), address, phone and email **for customers**. The form says what customers see (§4) and that details and payouts are reported to tax authorities where the law requires it (§9, DAC7), and needs the **self-certification** tick (DSA Art. 30). **Numbers are checked** (owner, 2026-10-14: "should be valid"; `packages/shared/src/company-ids.ts`): each country's format and official check digit — Polish NIP/PESEL/REGON/KRS, German USt-IdNr./tax IdNr/Steuernummer/HRB, Austrian UID/Steuernummer/FN, Swiss UID, North Macedonian EDB/EMBG/EMBS, Albanian NIPT, Kosovo 8–10 digits (no published format); stored in one form. Then the **official register** (function `company-registry`): VIES for EU VAT numbers, the Polish VAT white list, the Swiss UID register — a name that isn't the typed one, or a VAT number/UID the register doesn't know, is refused; a Polish NIP not on the white list (not VAT-registered) and a register that doesn't answer are saved and checked by hand. Kosovo, Albania and North Macedonia have no register API (ARBK, QKB, CRM only have search websites): **owner chose checking by hand, free**, against the company extract (step 2). Every answer is kept (`company_registry_checks`) for the verification. Registration number: required in XK, MK, PL; optional in DE and AT (sole traders often aren't in the commercial register); the NIPT (AL) and UID (CH) are both numbers. **Address** (owner: "valid, with confirmation"): found in the map search (OpenStreetMap via `places`, now with street/number/postcode/city), then confirmed — "Yes, this is the address" — with a link to check it on the map; the mover adds the house number (often missing in Kosovo/Albania map data) and the street when the map has none. Stored as text, parts and a point; Postgres refuses an address without its point or outside the country of registration. **Phone** (owner: "a code to prove it"; chose SMS + WhatsApp): a 6-digit code by SMS or WhatsApp via **Twilio Verify** (function `phone-verification`; the owner opens the Twilio account; costs about $0.05 per check + the message), not Supabase's phone sign-in (it would make the phone a second login, and Supabase documents a phone_change mix-up). Confirmed numbers are kept in `phone_verifications` (server only); Postgres accepts the company's phone only from that list. Until Twilio exists, `PHONE_TEST_NUMBERS` give fixed codes to listed test numbers. Other rules in Postgres (migration 20261014090000): the server dates the self-certification once; switching business ↔ private person, like a change to name, numbers or countries, sends a verified company back to verification; movers can't set the verification or Stripe columns. Saving doesn't verify anything — MoversHub checks the company with the documents (step 2) in the admin panel (step 5), which must refuse to verify an incomplete company. The checklist row shows To do / Saved / Verified / Needs changes. **Open (lawyer/accountant):** DAC7 also needs a **date of birth** for EU-resident private sellers and, for some EU companies, a VAT number besides the tax number (e.g. Germany) — not asked yet. |
